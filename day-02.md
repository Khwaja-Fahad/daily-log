## Defensive vs offensive security
Offensive security means thinking like an attacker: finding weaknesses in
systems (for example, hidden pages) so they can be fixed. Defensive security
means protecting systems and detecting attacks: monitoring, investigating
alerts, and responding to incidents. Offence finds the holes, defence
watches and protects.

## What a SOC analyst does
A SOC (Security Operations Centre) analyst monitors alerts and logs from an
organisation's systems, decides which ones are real threats and which are
false alarms, investigates suspicious activity, and escalates or responds
when something is actually wrong.

## What surprised me in the FakeBank room
The simulation felt like real work. I detected a suspicious login attempt,
blocked it, and then wrote an incident report for the team. The report
included the attacker's IP address, the time of the attack, the victim
account, the login page that was targeted, and which analyst was on shift.
The explanations made it easy to follow.

What I learned: the job is not only stopping the attacker. First you
contain the threat (block it), then you document it so the team can
learn from it and respond properly.
